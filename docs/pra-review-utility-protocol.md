# Review usefulness and distinct valid points

Reassess the same three saved owl-paper primary reviews once. Generate no new manuscript reviews. This changes the evaluation rubric after the earlier results were seen and is explicitly exploratory. Do not replace the earlier judgments or count this as another paper or independent replication.

Primary outcome: the blind judge's holistic preference for the review that best helps improve scientific validity, interpretation, and reporting. Supporting outcomes: usefulness on an anchored 1 to 10 scale and six dimensions on anchored 1 to 5 scales, with concrete rationales. Dimensions are scientific accuracy, importance, actionability, calibration, coverage, and prioritization. The overall score is not computed by adding point counts or averaging dimensions.

The judge decomposes each review comment into independently addressable scientific points. Supporting observations, restatements, alternative remedies, and consequences do not become extra issues. Each point must quote its exact review excerpt and receive a validity and importance assessment grounded in manuscript evidence. Valid concerns and consequential clarifications count separately from partly valid, unsupported, and unverifiable points. Semantic grouping prevents duplicate formulations within a review from earning repeated credit. Every source finding and point must be accounted for.

The judge sees all three anonymous reports and the same source evidence, without workflow identities, token costs, or prior judgments. Labels fixed before this new call: A tool-free, B ensemble, C ordinary workspace. Compare every pair and provide an overall preference with ties allowed. The ranking is not forced to follow counts or scores. Only primary reports are in scope.

Use one Terra medium subscription call, a 200,000 observed-token launch ceiling, no tools, no automatic retries, no fallback model, and no paid credits or reset. Invalid point references remain unscored and make preferences provisional. Preserve protocol, exact inputs, raw response, source receipts, and earlier assessments. One judge's confidence or numerical score is not a calibrated measure of truth.
